\section{Digital Twin}\label{sec:twin}

\subsection{Scene and Plant Models}

The plant is simulated in MuJoCo~\cite{Todorov2012mujoco} with a 2\,ms physics step
(Fig.~\ref{fig:twin}). The scene contains the 6-axis arm with a suction tool, a 2100\,mm belt,
the three part types, six bins, two photo-eyes, the inspection camera and a second camera for
demonstration videos. The cell geometry has a single source: the arm, belt frame, photo-eyes and
bin positions are generated from the PLC's constant list \texttt{GVL\_CellGeometry}, so the twin
and the PLC kinematics cannot drift apart.

Each robot joint is a simulated servo drive: PD position control with velocity feed-forward,
torque limits (300\,N\,m on the main axes, 60\,N\,m on the wrist), rotor inertia and viscous
friction. The setpoint received from the PLC is interpolated over the bridge cycle, and the twin
returns measured joint positions and torques. Parts lying on the belt are moved by the change of
the PLC's conveyor position, so the twin's parts and the PLC's part tracking use the same belt.
Suction is modelled as a weld constraint that is activated when the vacuum valve is on and a
downward ray from the cup finds a part within 5\,mm; the vacuum switch reports the weld back to
the PLC. Photo-eyes are ray casts across the belt, and each bin counts its parts and reports
\emph{full} at a configured capacity.

\subsection{Kinematic Consistency}

The joint directions and the orientation convention of the twin were measured against CODESYS
SoftMotion (Z-Y-Z Euler angles of the tool frame in machine coordinates). Eleven reference poses
captured from SoftMotion are compared with the twin's forward kinematics in a unit test; they
agree within 0.5\,mm and 0.1\,°.

\subsection{Bridge Cycle}

The bridge and the twin run in one Python process with a 20\,ms cycle. In each cycle the bridge
reads the PLC outputs (joint setpoints with their sample number, conveyor, vacuum valve, capture
request, state) and writes the previous cycle's inputs in the same round trip, steps the twin ten
times 2\,ms, spawns the parts due by belt travel, passes captures to the vision worker, and
prepares the next inputs: measured joints with the setpoint sample they answer, photo-eyes, vacuum
switch, bin-full signals, door, E-stop and heartbeat. Windows timing needed care on both
sides: the runner paces its cycle on a high-resolution counter, and the soft-PLC process is
exempted from the timer throttling of Windows~11, under which its 4\,ms task ran only every
15.6\,ms.

\subsection{Scenarios and Fault Injection}

A scenario file sets the random seed, the run duration, the bin capacity and the part supply:
one part per 1000\,mm of belt travel ($\pm$100\,mm), an equal class mix, a lateral offset of up
to $\pm$50\,mm and a yaw of up to $\pm$30\,°. Faults are injected at given times with a given
duration: E-stop, open door, full bin, lost bridge, dropped part and blocked joint. Recovery is
left to the operator, through the HMI or, in automated tests, through OPC UA. When the cell
leaves Execute because of a fault, a flight recorder logs the last 50 bridge cycles.
