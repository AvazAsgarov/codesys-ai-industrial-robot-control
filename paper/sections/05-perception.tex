\section{Perception}\label{sec:perception}

\subsection{Parts and Camera}

The part types are three scanned products from the Google Scanned Objects collection (CC~BY~4.0),
chosen so that they can be told apart from above: a probiotic box (class~0), a vitamin jar
(class~1) and a coffee can (class~2). Their meshes and textures are placed in the twin, and the
PLC holds the grip height of each class.

The inspection camera looks at the belt at photo-eye PE1 from a distance of 750\,mm, tilted
25\,° from the vertical (1280$\times$720 pixels, 45\,° vertical field of view), and is rendered
offscreen by MuJoCo. Pixels are mapped to the belt plane with a pinhole model whose intrinsics
and pose come from a checkerboard calibration in the twin (OpenCV \texttt{calibrateCamera} and
\texttt{solvePnP}, 15 seeded board poses). Because the twin knows the true camera, the calibration
can be scored: the reprojection error is 0.06\,px RMS, the focal length is within 0.02\,\%, and
the camera position and rotation are within 1.2\,mm and 0.09\,°.

\subsection{Detection Without Training}

The detector is YOLOE-26s-seg~\cite{Wang2025yoloe,Sapkota2026yoloe26}, an open-vocabulary
instance-segmentation model, run on the laptop CPU through OpenVINO (FP32). The project's design
rule was not to train or fine-tune a detector. Text prompts were tried first, but on the rendered
products they reached only 0.138 mAP@0.5 (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}). The cell therefore uses
\emph{visual prompts}: six seeded reference scenes are rendered from the twin, the box of each
example is given to YOLOE, the prompt embeddings are averaged per class and folded into the
network, and the network is exported to OpenVINO. No weights are trained, and a new part type
needs only its mesh. Text prompts and the COCO-trained YOLO26~\cite{Ultralytics2026yolo26} are
kept as baselines.

The detector sees a 640$\times$640 window around PE1 at native resolution, in which a part is
about 150\,px wide. From the mask of the chosen detection, the centroid gives the belt position
at the part's top height, and a minimum-area rectangle of the mask outline on the belt plane gives
the orientation. When two labels overlap with an IoU of at least 0.7, only the more confident
one is kept. The belt travel between the capture and the rendered snapshot is subtracted, so
the pose refers to the belt position latched by the PLC at the capture.

\subsection{Integration with the Control Cycle}

Inference takes about 170\,ms on the CPU, far longer than the 20\,ms bridge cycle. At each
capture request the bridge therefore passes only a snapshot of the twin state (a few hundred
numbers) to a vision worker thread, which renders the frame and runs the detector; the bridge
writes the result to \texttt{GVL\_Bridge.stVision} in the first cycle after it is ready and never
waits for it.
