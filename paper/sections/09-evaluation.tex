\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation has three parts: unit tests of the PLC application in CODESYS's own simulation,
end-to-end runs of the soft-PLC with the twin, and a benchmark of the vision pipeline on rendered
frames. All runs used one laptop (Intel Core Ultra 7 255U, 16\,GB RAM, no discrete GPU) and the
default process parameters, which equal the \emph{Standard} recipe (belt 100\,mm/s).

\subsection{PLC Tests in Simulation}

Each test case is a JSON file of steps run by a script against the CODESYS simulation, which
uses the real compiler and SoftMotion libraries: write variables, wait a number of cycles or
milliseconds, and compare variables with expected values. Test programs (\texttt{PRG\_Test*})
inject parts, vision and decision messages, vacuum signals and faults where a block is tested
without the twin. Time-based cases wait in real time; because the simulation slows down under CPU
load, the waits have margin and the expectations check steady states. Table~\ref{tab:plctests}
lists the 77 cases in 12 files.

\subsection{End-to-End Runs on the Soft-PLC}

For the end-to-end runs the application was downloaded to CODESYS Control Win V3 x64 (4\,ms
\texttt{MainTask}) and connected to the twin over OPC UA. Production was started with Reset and
Start, from the HMI in the 20-minute run and through OPC UA in the automated tests. The bridge
reports, per run, the parts spawned or captured, the parts placed in each bin, parts placed in a
bin other than the one matching their true class (\emph{wrong}), parts that ran off the end of
the belt unpicked (\emph{missed}), parts that fell anywhere else (\emph{lost}), and the longest gap
between two bridge cycles, which must stay below the PLC's 500\,ms bridge watchdog.
Table~\ref{tab:e2e} summarises the production runs. In all of them the decision layer was off and
the PLC sorted by class.

In the 20-minute live run the camera and YOLOE answered every capture. Of the 87 captured
parts, 78 were placed in the bins of their classes (30, 23 and 25 parts); no part was placed in a
wrong bin, missed or lost. The bridge cycle (20\,ms period) took 8.1\,ms on average and 13.0\,ms
at the 95th percentile, and the longest gap between two cycles was 256\,ms, about half of the
watchdog time. The longest gaps grew from 39\,ms with the ground-truth stand-in to
199--256\,ms when the camera rendering and the detector ran in the same process as the bridge;
the vision load therefore appears to set the timing margin of the link on this laptop, although
the runs do not isolate the cause.

\emph{Fault injection.} A second automated test runs the fault scenario of
Section~\ref{sec:twin} on the soft-PLC: an E-stop at 60\,s, a full Type~A bin at 120\,s, a lost
bridge (heartbeat stopped for 1\,s) at 180\,s and an opened door at 240\,s, with ground-truth
vision. Each fault was detected: the E-stop, the lost bridge and the door aborted the cell, and
the full bin suspended it. After the lost bridge, the newest alarm reported by the PLC was the
following-error fault of the frozen twin rather than \emph{BridgeLost}, since both trip within
the same watchdog time. After each abort the operator sequence (Clear, Reset, Start, played by
the test through OPC UA) brought the cell back to Execute, and the suspended cell resumed by
itself when the injected bin fault ended after 15\,s. No part was lost; a part held during an abort was
placed in Quarantine on restart, as designed. The fault test also found a real defect during
development: after a SoftMotion lag error, Clearing could hang because the group reset reported
``no error to reset''; \texttt{FB\_Robot} now handles this case (Section~\ref{sec:plc}).

\subsection{Vision Benchmark}

The vision pipeline was scored on 500 frames rendered from the twin with 1--3 parts each near
PE1 (951 parts: 284 boxes, 358 jars, 309 cans), random yaw, seeded lighting, noise and blur
(Table~\ref{tab:vision}). Ground truth (classes, masks, belt poses) is exact. A detection
matches a part when the class agrees and the box IoU is at least 0.5; position errors are
measured on the belt plane at a confidence of at least 0.25, and latency is the detector call
alone on the CPU (OpenVINO FP32). Prompt wording and camera tilt were tuned on another seed (300);
the test seed (301) was run once.

With visual prompts the detector reaches 0.997 mAP@0.5 without any training, against a design
threshold of 0.80 below which the part set or the detector would have had to change. Text
prompts fail on these products seen from above (0.138); turning the image by 90\,° so that the
upright parts appear upright raises them to 0.566, and YOLO26 has no COCO class for a box or a
can (0.058). The position error is dominated by geometry, not by the detector: even the true
masks give 1.8\,mm, because the centroid of a tilted view is not the centroid of the footprint.
This is small compared with the suction cup radius of 20\,mm. The box orientation error is
1.0\,° at the 95th percentile, but about 3\,\% of the nearly square boxes are reported 90\,° off;
the suction cup does not need the orientation to pick. The p50 latency of 166\,ms is decoupled
from the bridge cycle by the vision worker (Section~\ref{sec:perception}).

\subsection{Limitations}

All results come from simulation. The images are renders of the twin, and the visual prompts are
built from renders of the same twin, so the benchmark shows that the pipeline works without
training, not that it transfers to real camera images. The soft-PLC runs on Windows without
hard real-time guarantees and under a demonstration licence that stops it after about two hours;
the safety logic is modelled, not certified; the OPC UA link is unauthenticated and limited to
the local host. The end-to-end figures come from single runs with one seed each, so no
confidence intervals are given, and only the \emph{Standard} recipe was run end to end. The
decision layer was not exercised in the cell, and the twin does not yet produce defective parts,
so quality sorting and the planned comparison of the rule baseline with the decision layer
remain open.
